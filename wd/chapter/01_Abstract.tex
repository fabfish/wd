\begin{abstract}
Weight decay introduces a hyperparameter $\lambda$ that improves generalization but disrupts the balance among optimization hyperparameters (learning rate $\eta$, batch size $B$), making optimal tuning substantially more difficult. While prior work has focused on scaling laws for $\eta$ and $B$, $\lambda$ is typically treated as a fixed constant, overlooking its impact on optimization dynamics. In this work, we systematically study how $\lambda$ affects the stability of the optimization system, as well as the optimal choices of $\eta$ and $B$. Using a stability-based analysis, we derive generalization bounds for basic optimizers such as stochastic gradient descent (SGD), showing that weight decay can tighten upper bounds but also imposes stricter constraints on $\eta$. We further propose a heuristic regularization equivalence hypothesis, under which we derive the analytic scaling law $\lambda \approx \frac{1}{\eta T}$, consistent with recent empirical results that interpret AdamW as an exponential moving average (EMA). Extending this analysis to the batch size, we show that the optimal product $\lambda\eta$ scales with $B$. Experiments across diverse architectures, from ResNet-18 on vision tasks to Qwen3-0.6B on language modeling, validate our theoretical and heuristic findings. 

\end{abstract}
